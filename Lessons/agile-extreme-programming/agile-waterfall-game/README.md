### Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
* Describe the difference between the waterfall and Agile methodologies.
* Explain the roles and challenges of product owners and developers.

## Introduction (5 min)

### The Challenge

You'll be working in a small team comprising one product owner and several developers. With your team, you'll have 15 minutes to build a product from a Lego kit.

Your product owner should know what needs to be built. Your developers should have the Lego bricks and must rely on the product owner to know what to build.

---

## The Challenge (20 min)

### Forming Teams and Assigning Roles

We'll divide the class into teams of five people. Some groups will be "Agile" while some will be "waterfall." 

> **Instructor Note**: Don't tell the groups which they are.

One product owner will be assigned for each team; the rest will be developers.

#### Product Owner and Developers 

![agile-waterfall-game](./Lego.png)

### Assigning Waterfall and Agile Product Owners

Product owners should come to the front of the room for the materials and instructions. 

> **Instructor Note**: If the room is small, conduct this activity outside of the room.

Half of the product owners will be assigned as waterfall, while the other half will be Agile. 

> **Instructor Note**: Don't let the rest of the class hear.

Each product owner will receive one Lego kit. They'll have three minutes to study and memorize their kit's instructions, which must remain at the front of the room. Product owners cannot show the instructions to the developers in their team.

After studying their Lego kit instructions, product owners will return to their teams with the Lego kit. Each team will have 15 minutes to build their product using the assigned methodology.

---

### Facilitating the Workflows

Teams have a total of 15 minutes to work. Waterfall and Agile teams will follow a different pace.

### Team Workflows

For the **waterfall** teams:

* Pace the waterfall teams in five-minute increments.
*	Let waterfall teams know when each five-minute increment has passed.
*	During the first five minutes, product owners should explain the product.
*	During the second and third five-minute increments, developers should build the product.

For the **Agile** teams:

*	Pace the Agile teams in three five-minute sprints.
*	Let Agile teams know when each sprint begins and ends. For each sprint, call out the start and end of the first-minute mark.
*	During minute one of each sprint, product owners should explain the product and provide feedback.
*	During minutes two through five, developers should build the product.
*	Repeat the cycle for a total of three five-minute sprints within the allotted 15 minutes.

### Waterfall Team Instructions

During the first five minutes of their 15-minute project, waterfall product owners will explain the project to the developers. Product owners may explain only with words; they may not show drawings or instructions.

After explaining the project, the product owners must return to the front of the room. Developers then have 10 minutes to build the project. 

Product owners must remain at the front of the room. They're prohibited from giving support, guidance, or feedback during development.

### Agile Team Instructions

Agile teams will work in three five-minute sprints. During minute one of each sprint, product owners will explain the product and provide feedback. Product owners may explain only with words; they may not show drawings or instructions. After each one-minute round of explanation or feedback, the product owner must return to the front of the room.

During minutes two through five of each sprint, developers will build the product. Developers will have four minutes of build time.

During the four-minute development phases, product owners must stay at the front of the room and are prohibited from giving support, guidance, or feedback.

Product owners and developers repeat this cycle a total of three times during the 15-minute project.

---

## Brief Debrief (5 min)

As a group, answer the questions below to share key takeaways and discuss the results of the exercise. Be sensitive to avoid defining waterfall as "bad."
*	What was easy or hard about your assigned way of working?
*	In which way did your team work?
*	What could have been done to improve your results?
*	What do you consider to be the pros and cons of each approach?
