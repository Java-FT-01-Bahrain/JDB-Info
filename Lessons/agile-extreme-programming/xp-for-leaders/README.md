# Extreme Programming for Leaders

## Learning Objectives

- Plan for success in an extreme programming environment.
- Manage stakeholders and escalate issues as needed.
- Implement extreme programming principles within your team.

## Lesson Overview 

| Topic | Type | Timing |
| --- | --- | -- |
| Opening | Intro | 5 min |
| The Role of a Leader in Extreme Programming | Exercise | 20 min |
| Planning for Success | Lecture | 30 min |
| Scenario Time | Exercise | 20 min |
| Wrap Up | Conclusion | 10 min |

## Opening (5 min)

Extreme programming strikes a balance between a centralized leadership structure that controls every aspect of the project and a completely decentralized leadership structure in which everyone sets their own course and makes their own decisions.

Let's think about how other teams work. They may include product managers, project managers, and lead programmers who make decisions and control the process and outcomes. In these teams, managers typically own the process and the project, while programmers own the code. Decisions follow a hierarchical path from manager to developer.

Extreme programming teams are lean and flexible, with collective ownership of the process and code. Work is based on communication, simplicity, feedback, and courage. In this type of environment, good relationships and trust are the keys to success.

## The Role of the Leader in Extreme Programming (20 min)

Before we get into leadership, let's recap: What are some of the key principles of extreme programming?

<!--**Instructor Note**: Have people shout things out and explain what they mean.-->

Given the unique environment of an extreme programming team, the role of a leader on one of these teams is also unique. So, let's think through what that looks like!

Here's what some of those responsibilities might entail:

- Highlighting what needs to be done.
- Enabling developers and the customer to do their best work.
- Removing blockers or long meetings and reports that prevent the team from doing their work.
- Keeping track of project progress and velocity.
- Communicating project progress and status to the team.
- Suggesting changes if progress is slow or behind schedule.
- Intervening with a decision if the team cannot resolve a problem or issue in the usual distributed manner.

#### Discussion

Take a moment to discuss with a partner: How does this look different from a manager's role in a "regular" (non-extreme programming) team?

<!--**Instructor Note**: Have people share out some ideas after they discuss.-->

### Building Relationships

In an extreme programming environment, leadership rests on **accepted responsibility**. Accepted responsibility comes from building good relationships with your team, stakeholders, and leaders.

#### Reflection

Maybe you've managed some engineering teams. Or maybe you've never managed anyone! Either way, take a moment to jot down some ideas on **how you could build strong relationships as a leader**.

You might want to consider...

1. Who was your favorite manager? What did they do that you loved?
2. Who was the least effective manager you had? Why didn't it work out?
3. How would you want to be perceived as a manager?
4. How can you help people stay motivated at work?

## Planning for Success (20 min)

Extreme programming projects embrace change. That doesn’t mean that the team needs to fly by the seat of its pants. You can put structures in place and follow best practices to help ensure success.

Here are some best practices we believe help leaders in extreme programming environments (or any work environment!).

<!--**Instructor Note**: Introduce each practice and lead students through a discussion around how each one works.-->

#### Evaluate the project before it starts to make sure it’s a good fit for extreme programming.

I know we've been singing its praises, but not all projects are suitable for extreme programming.

Take a moment to consider: What are some factors that might *not* make a project right for extreme programming?

You should ask yourself questions such as...

- Can the business culture support the values and process of extreme programming (e.g., simplicity, accepted responsibility, communication, decentralized process)?
- Is the project the right size for extreme programming? A team of 100, 50, or even 20 is probably too large; 10 is the magic number.
- What technologies are you using? Will the technologies support the extreme programming process?
- Will you be able to test your code quickly and easily to get feedback in a timely manner?
- Does your physical space support extreme programming? Is there a shared, open space on one floor for the team, or will members be isolated in separate cubicles (or even worse, on separate floors)?

#### Deal with fires in a timely manner.

It's hard to anticipate everything that might happen, but it's helpful to think through the worst-case scenario and anticipate how things might play out.

How about situations like these?

- A key resource becomes unavailable.
- There is a serious error in the code.
- Tasks are consistently late.
- A team member resists the extreme programming process.
- Team members are not respectful toward each other.
- The customer is unavailable or not providing timely, accurate information.

#### Work with stakeholders.

Working with stakeholders is important for any role, but especially for managers.

What are some best practices?

- Know your stakeholders. Make sure the project has clearly identified, committed stakeholders.
- Introduce the team and extreme programming process to stakeholders.
- Have a well-established communication plan for stakeholders with an agreed-upon frequency and mode of communication.
- Treat the customer with respect and as a partner in the development process.

#### Escalate issues in a timely manner.

When something goes wrong, it's not productive to sit around and hope that it goes away. Escalating issues is a key function of leaders.

How can you do that efficiently?

- Have an escalation procedure in place. Know when and how to escalate issues.
- Create an escalation matrix to identify the types of problems that require escalation. Problems should be tagged by severity.
- Make sure your matrix includes a schedule for escalation and resolution.

## Scenario Time (30 min)

Enough talk about leading and extreme programming. Let's put it into practice!

We'll get into groups of 3–4 and walk through some scenarios. Below are a few example issues that may come up during an extreme programming project. With your group, discuss:
- What signs indicate the issue is brewing?
- What steps should be taken to solve the issue?
- Who should take the lead in solving the issue?
- What preventive steps or measures should be taken to avoid similar issues in the future?

Try to get through as many of these issues as you can in the next 20 minutes. Then, we'll take time to discuss the issues and your solutions as a class.

1. A pair of programmers is not being productive. It appears as if there is friction between them. What should the team do?
2. The customer assigned to the team is rarely available. What should the team do?
3. The customer consistently provides incorrect information. What should the team do?
4. The customer is not respectful of the programmers. They are constantly interrupting and often come up with requests for more and more features. What should the team do?
5. One of the most experienced programmers needs to work remotely for a period of time. What should the team do?
6. The customer is suddenly pulled from the project. What should the team do?
7. One of the most experienced programmers on the project is suddenly pulled from the team. What should the team do?
8. You are the manager or the most experienced developer on the team. You will be leaving the organization at a critical time for the project. How do you prepare your team?
9. Your organization has followed traditional Waterfall methodology. The director of application development wants to try Agile/XP. They have asked you to lead this initiative. What should you do?
10. The team has many members who work fully remotely. You're now shifting toward an extreme programming approach to development. How will it work for them?

## Conclusion (5 min)

We can talk at length about how to lead extreme programming teams, but most of this knowledge will come from your own experience.

As we wrap up, let's take a moment to reflect and discuss:

- What do you think makes extreme programming hard?
- What do you think makes extreme programming easy?
